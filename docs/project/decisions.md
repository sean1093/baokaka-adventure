# Decision Log

Append-only. Each entry: id, date, decision, why, consequences. To change a decision, add a new entry that supersedes the old one (and mark the old one "Superseded by D-xx"). Decisions D-01 to D-10 were made by the owner; D-11 onward were made by the project/program manager while planning the work (2026-10-18) and can be revisited by the owner.

---

### D-01 · 2026-10-18 · Keep only the RPG
**Decision.** The site stops being a collection. The hub, 找找看 (`src/find`) and 圖案數獨 (`src/sudoku`) are removed. The RPG (`src/quest`) becomes the single game and the site boots straight into its title screen.
**Why.** Owner decision: one deep, polished game instead of three small ones.
**Consequences.** Hub/find/sudoku code, tests, routes, sprites used only by them, and their storage keys (`baokaka.progress`, `baokaka.sudoku`) go away (issue #2). Shared utilities still used by the RPG (`shared/storage.ts`, `shared/random.ts`, `shared/audio.ts`, `components/*`) stay.

### D-02 · 2026-10-18 · Full story rewrite and new title
**Decision.** The 寶咖咖奇俠傳 / 仙劍 setting is replaced entirely by 《寶咖咖抓周大冒險》 — *Baokaka & Mocha: Midnight Across Taipei* (`docs/story/story-bible.md`, `docs/story/script.md`). 抓周 (the first-birthday grab ceremony) is the spine: nine treasures plus the gold lock, the red string, the 「1」 candle and the blank invitation.
**Why.** Owner request for a warm, family story rooted in Taipei that grandparents and kids both understand.
**Consequences.** Every map, event, foe, item and most sprites are new content. Terminology changes: 銅板 (coins, was 貼紙), 元氣 (MP, was 真氣), 才藝 (skills, was 仙術), 武力/身手 (was 武術/身法). Battle tone: tantrums, not violence (no 殺/死/血).

### D-03 · 2026-10-18 · 14 chapters in one night
**Decision.** 14 chapters across Taipei landmarks from 00:00 to 05:30, each chapter card shows a clock. Ending at 05:30 (阿嬤 wakes) and 10:00 (the party). Locations: home/大安森林公園, 台大, 台北車站, 西門町, 龍山寺/剝皮寮, 大稻埕, 士林夜市, 松山機場, 動物園/貓空, 碧潭, 北投, 陽明山, 淡水, 台北101; post-game 象山.
**Why.** "The night is the timer" gives a readable sense of progress and a natural colour script (navy → indigo → violet → gold).

### D-04 · 2026-10-18 · Airport, expanded 淡水, playable MRT
**Decision.** Owner-requested additions: a 松山機場 chapter (Ch8, planes asleep 23:00–05:00, the 「飛機！」 word), 淡水 expanded with 漁人碼頭 and the new 淡江大橋 (opened 2026-05-12; drawn as a non-walkable backdrop), and MRT rides as **playable** moments (the 月亮線: route-map screen + 5 walkable ride cars themed after real lines) rather than a menu.
**Consequences.** Engine work for travel (issue #9) and a ride-car content issue (#27). Accuracy rules: civilian, unbranded airport; no light rail drawn on the bridge; no designer named.

### D-05 · 2026-10-18 · Expand to 6–8 hours
**Decision.** Grow the game from ~2 h to 6–8 h with sub-maps and gate puzzles per chapter, cross-chapter side quests (lost & found, deliveries, clerk points, 長輩圖, echoes, MRT riders), hero side stories with signature gear, collections, a bestiary and a post-game (象山 superboss, boss rush, quiz).
**Consequences.** Content volume roughly ×4. Each chapter is split into a content issue and an art issue so PRs stay reviewable.

**Consequences.** `MAX_LEVEL`, `XP_TABLE`, skill unlocks, foe stats from Ch6 on scaled ~×1.15 per chapter shift and settled by `sim.test.ts` (issues #5, #54).

### D-07 · 2026-10-18 · Topical-joke rules
**Decision.** Mostly evergreen Taiwanese life memes. Recent (2025–2026) memes only if verified by web search and light-hearted, each with a 12-month review date. No politics, no politicians, no real celebrities or brands, nothing mean or scary. Every joke must work for a grandparent and be harmless to a 6-year-old. Jokes sit only on optional NPCs/variants and never gate progress; each is removable by deleting one talk rule.
**Consequences.** The registry is story bible § 9. The only recent joke at the time of writing is `gag.ch13.newBridge` (review 2027-10). QA checks the registry in issue #58.

### D-20 · 2026-10-18 · Save v2 details: reading never writes, and where UI strings live
**Decision.** (Issue #3.) `loadQuest()` only reads: it returns `{ save, legacyReset }`, and the old `baokaka.quest` key is deleted by `discardLegacyQuest()`, which `QuestGame` calls in an effect after mount. The issue sketched `loadQuest` deleting the key itself, but React StrictMode calls state initialisers twice in development, and the second call would no longer see the key and drop the notice. The stored record is `{ version: 2, run, sound, settings: { skipRides } }` under `baokaka.quest.v2`; a valid v2 record wins over a leftover legacy key, and a corrupt v2 record silently becomes a fresh run without the notice. `VictorySummary` gained `boss: FoeId | null` so the victory screen can say 「〈boss名〉不暈了！」. Engine/system strings (battle log, victory, defeat, inn, shop, stat names, command labels, the reset notice) live in `src/quest/engine/strings.ts` (`UI`); story dialogue stays in the event scripts. Coins are counted with 個 (「銅板不夠，還差 N 個。」, shop prices 「N 個」). The world screen's home button is now labelled 回到標題 (it returns to the title since #2), and the defeat tip says 體力少 instead of 血少 (tone guide: no 血).
**Why.** A pure load keeps the one-time notice correct in every React mode; one strings module lets the M2 re-skin and later chapters change wording in one place.
**Consequences.** Any later incompatible `Run` change bumps `SAVE_VERSION` and the key the same way and treats the previous v-key as legacy (never migrate across stories). New system-level text goes into `UI`, not inline JSX.

### D-21 · 2026-10-18 · Visual QA tooling details
**Decision.** (Issue #4.) `--serve` in `scripts/visual-check.mjs` starts a Vite **dev** server rather than `vite preview` as the issue sketched: the art lab is dev-only by design, so a production preview has no art lab targets to shoot (`--url` at a preview still works and runs only `title`). Art lab fixtures are starting states rendered through a new `src/quest/QuestScreens.tsx` (the view switch extracted from `QuestGame`, which keeps storage and music), over a live `questReducer`, so screenshots show the real wiring and a fixture can be played on. The script discovers fixture names from the art lab index (`[data-fixture]`) instead of duplicating them in `visual-targets.mjs`; duplicate target names abort the run (the `title` target is the real `#/`, the fixture with a saved run is `title-continue`). Silhouettes are a black box masked by the sprite's own markup (`renderToStaticMarkup` → data-URL `mask-image`), so no filter is involved. The `artlab-sprites-night` target uses `size=64` so the full-page sheet stays a sane height (≈3,400 CSS px); the lab itself shows all four sizes. Screenshots wait for `document.fonts.ready` and a 0.5 s DOM-quiet period (covers the typewriter dialogue) and use Playwright's `animations: 'disabled'` on top of `?still=1`. Dev dependencies are pinned exactly: `playwright@1.56.1` (matches the preinstalled Chromium build 1194), `pixelmatch@7.1.0`, `pngjs@7.0.0`. Art lab backgrounds use literal dev-only swatches (`#0B1238` night, a brick pattern), not palette tokens.
**Why.** Keeps the art lab out of production while making `npm run visual` a single command, and keeps one registry per thing (sprites, fixtures) so later issues only add entries.
**Consequences.** Baselines depend on installed fonts until #10 ships web fonts: committed baselines come from the agent environment; humans on another OS regenerate locally. `--perf` measures rAF intervals on the dev build, which are vsync-floored at 16.7 ms and pessimistic; #57 should switch to a trace-based frame-work measurement against a production build if it needs the § 8.5 12 ms figure.

### D-23 · 2026-10-18 · Script steps v2 details
**Decision.** (Issue #6.) `choice` and `goEvent` replace the *whole* rest of the scene, and `include` splices its event inline (both rewrite `scene.steps`/`at`, never recurse). So that a branch can never silently cut off the event that included it, `validate.ts` rejects a `choice` or `goEvent` inside an included event (directly), as well as include cycles and nesting deeper than 4. `heal` with a ratio rounds up (`ceil(ratio × max)`) so "at least ratio" holds, and `ratio: 1` is exactly the old full heal. A labelled chapter card (`尾聲`, `番外`) still needs an `n` (use 0 or 15; it is ignored for `run.chapter`); the card shows the label instead of 「第 n 章」. `inn 0` is handled in `runScene` (heal + a fixed line, `UI.innFree`), so the reducer has no free-inn branch. Choice buttons are all the same weight (no primary), because the post-game quiz must not hint the answer. The art-lab fixture `chapter` was renamed `chapter-card` (with a 00:00 clock) to match the issue; `dialogue-choice` is new. `runScene` gives up (`done`) after 64 `goEvent`/`include` expansions in one call, as a guard against a runaway `goEvent` loop that validation does not try to detect.
**Why.** Keeps the runner pure and flat as the issue asks, and moves the one sharp edge of mixing sub-calls with branches into the content linter.
**Consequences.** #21 writes the new chapters with `time` on every card; #9 fills `substitute()` in `src/quest/engine/text.ts`; #12 restyles `ChapterCard` and `ChoicePrompt`.

### D-24 · 2026-10-18 · Progress data: derived flags, quests, 圖鑑, escorts
**Decision.** (Issue #7.) All progress tables live in `src/quest/engine/progress.ts` (`DERIVED`, `QUESTS`, `TRAY`, `COLLECTIONS`, `BESTIARY_FOES`, `FOLLOWERS`) and are pure data over `run.flags` + `run.seenFoes`. Every engine path that sets flags goes through `addFlags` (script `flag` steps, chests, triggers) and victories go through `recordFoes`; both end in `refreshDerived`, which runs `applyDerived` to a fixed point. `bestiaryAll` is the one derived flag computed from `seenFoes` (every foe in `BESTIARY_FOES`, i.e. the roster minus `Foe.postGame` foes); it is checked first, so DERIVED entries may build on it, and it cannot have a DERIVED entry itself. Besides the bible's list, DERIVED seeds `lfAll` (the five `lf_*Back` flags, script § sq.lostFound) and four "at least one" flags, `clerkAny`, `elderAny`, `echoAny`, `riderAny` plus `lfAny`, so collect-in-any-order quests have a first step and `questStatus` keeps the bible's rule (started = first step set, next hint = first unset step, done = last step set) without special cases. Single-flag quests (`sq.bear`, `sq.callaLily`) only ever show as done; `sq.queue` and `sq.swanRace` start on the main-story flags that open them (`cutletDone`, `swanDone`). Collections store a title plus the page's line(s): stamps use the printed name and the stamp's picture, 長輩圖 the picture and its words, 回音 the place and both lines (no speaker, since `cloud`/`shushu` depends on `metShushu`), riders the name and the car. `Run.seenFoes` is required in a save, so the save moved to `version: 4` under `baokaka.quest.v4` and v3 joined `LEGACY_QUEST_KEYS` (D-20). Validation: DERIVED cycles, duplicates and bad `atLeast` are errors, as are repeated quest ids/step flags, hints over 24 characters, collection lines over 40, a ring that is not 9 treasures, `Foe.calmed` over 40 and `Foe.home` over 12; flags the tables read that no event sets yet are warnings (`progressWarnings()`, printed once in dev), which #58 turns into errors. Escorts: the world screen draws `activeFollower(flags)` from its own queue of the leader's walked tiles, one tile behind the last hero; after a map change it starts on `restingSpot` (screen-right of the party first, then screen-left, so it neither covers nor hides behind anyone) and waits there until the party has walked far enough to trail. The art lab passes a stand-in table (a snail under the flag `artlabFollower`) to `QuestScreens` because `FOLLOWERS` ships empty.
**Why.** One write path means no flag change can forget the derived flags; data-only pages let #19/#20 render from a save alone; the `…Any` flags keep the quest rule uniform.
**Consequences.** Chapter issues add their puzzle gates to DERIVED, their escorts to FOLLOWERS (`chickFollowing`, `dumplingFollowing`, `calfFollowing`), `calmed`/`home` per foe with the new roster, and set the flags the warnings list. The Ch14 issue may add a `bp_*` flag to the backpacker finale if the log should show a step there. When the new foe roster lands, mark 象山-only foes (`shushuBonus`) `postGame: true`.

### D-26 · 2026-10-18 · 月亮線 engine: stations as strings, arrival-only triggers, the `cleared` flag, save v6
**Decision.** (Issue #9.) `STATIONS` (in `src/quest/engine/mrt.ts`, with the stylised network `MRT_LINES`) holds all 14 rows of bible § 3.1 from now on, with `map`/`car` typed as plain strings. A station is drawn and can be lit before its map exists, but it can only be ridden once both its map (with `mrtArrival`) and its car exist (`rideable`); `mrtWarnings()` lists the ones that can't yet, inside `progressWarnings()`. `validateMrt` checks every station whose map exists. The art lab and the 路線圖 can show the full network now, and each chapter issue only adds its map. Riding works like this. `{ do: 'travel' }` stops the scene on `view: { screen: 'travel', fresh, note }`. Stations lit since the last opening are appended to `seenStations` right away and show `mrt.newStation` once. `ride` sets `rideTo` and puts the party on the car's entry tile. With `settings.skipRides` it puts them straight on the destination's `mrtArrival`. Either way the arrival tile's trigger plays, followed by whatever was left of the scene. The car door is `Exit.to: '@rideTo'` (no `at`), and it clears `rideTo`. If a car door is walked with no ride on record, the 路線圖 opens instead of leaving the party stuck. Two engine choices the bible doesn't spell out:

### D-42 · 2026-10-19 · Walk graph: gates, blocking and tie-breaks
**Decision.** (#81.) `src/popup/core/graph.ts`: (1) **A gate blocks the node attached to it.** GDD § 2.2.3 says a closed gate makes a node untraversable "as data conditions" without naming the field; a node with `attach: { part: <gate part> }` (the doorway, same position in both states) is blocked while that part is `closed`. No new field in the § 3.2 contract; pages may still put `when` on edges instead. (2) A creature blocks the node of its current position unless `blocks: false`; `offpage` and unknown nodes block nothing. Blocking applies to Baokaka only (Mocha's limits are perch-level, #83), and the start node never counts as blocked. (3) **"Ties broken by edge order"** = among shortest paths, the one whose sequence of edge indices from the start is lexicographically smallest; a BFS that expands each node's edges in data order (either direction keeps the edge's rank) gives exactly that. (4) `nearestReachableTo` compares squared distances (exact in integer page units), then path length, then id by code-unit order (`<`). (5) API: `nodePos`, `openEdges(def, state, walker)`, `blockedNodes`, and `walkGraph(def, state, walker)` → `WalkGraph` with `bfs`, `reachSet` (BFS order + steps) and `nearestReachableTo` (node id or point); a `Walker` is `{ who: 'baby', growth }` or `{ who: 'cat' }` because growth isn't in `SimState` (the page decides it). Edges with an unknown end or kind are ignored (lint reports them).
**Why.** Reusing `attach` keeps the contract unchanged and puts the gate's doorway where the art already needs it; the lexicographic rule is the natural, cheap reading of "edge order" and is what BFS gives for free.
**Consequences.** Page authors model a fare gate as a doorway node attached to the gate. Topology is compiled once per `PageDef` and cached by identity, so defs must be treated as immutable (PageLab's editor must create a new def per edit). #82 uses `reachSet`/`nearestReachableTo`/`bfs`; #83 uses the cat walker; the solver and PageLab overlays reuse `openEdges` and `blockedNodes`. Benchmark: ~340k walk-graph builds + BFS per second on a 40-node page (skipped test in `graph.test.ts`).

### D-44 · 2026-10-19 · Mocha: the readings GDD § 2.3 left open
**Decision.** (#83.) `src/popup/core/mocha.ts`: (1) **State.** `SimState.mocha` gains `to?` (the perch he is heading for) and `stare?` (the pushable of the eye-contact beat); mirrored in GDD § 3.2. (2) **Pathing.** Phase 1 (`mochaCommand`) computes a shortest cat path (`jump` + `squeeze`, conditions now, edge-order ties) from his current node and **commits** it: later edge changes don't strand him mid-air (C1). (3) **Refusals.** A perch on a `glass` node is always unreachable (`mochaNope`); one on a `wet` or `steam` node is unreachable while its `blockedWhen` holds — always, without one — and tapping it emits `mochaPuff` (「不要。」); any other perch with a true `blockedWhen` is unreachable (`mochaNope`). ### D-45 · 2026-10-19 · The tick: the readings GDD § 3.3 and § 2.4 left open
**Decision.** (#84.) `src/popup/core/step.ts` (`step`, `cascade`, `matchPredicate`), `words.ts`, `creatures.ts`, `replay.ts` and the full `events.ts`: (1) **Cascade.** FIFO; at most `MAX_CASCADE` = 32 § 3.2 events per cascade (UI events pass through uncounted), else `CascadeOverflowError` naming the page and the parts that entered a state more than once (else every part that entered one). ### D-46 · 2026-10-19 · The solver: the readings GDD § 3.7 left open
**Decision.** (#85.) `src/popup/core/solver.ts`, `solve(def, from = initialState(def), { cap, timeMs, clock, stopAtGoal })`: (1) **Key.** `solverKey(def, state)` is a compact string holding every `SimState` field except `tick` (it only grows) and `baby.reachTicks` (the bark beat, D-43: it changes nothing but itself and `bark` UI events, so states that differ only there have the same futures — a test checks this on every reachable c1p2 state). (2) **Inputs** (`candidateInputs`, in this order): `none`; for each unlocked part with a drag input (`drag-linear`, `drag-rotary`), a `set` to every other stop; for each unlocked part with a tap rule, a `tap` — **including tabs, sliders and wheels**, whose tap cycles (last → first) and can reach states a drag can't in one move; `mocha` to every perch with a cat path (not his own or his current destination); `pet` while he's settled and not staring; `say` for every word `wordAvailable` allows (not while carried or linked); `floor` to every height-0 node in linked mode. (4) **Par** = fewest non-wait inputs, by a 0-1 BFS (a FIFO per move count; wait edges stay in the layer). `trivial` = par 0. `solution` = the parent-pointer path to the first done state settled (waits as one `none` per tick; `toSolutionSteps` folds them into `{ wait: n }`). (7) **`nextAction(state)`** = the first input on a path with the fewest moves, **then the fewest ticks** (a Dijkstra over reversed edges, computed on first use), first in candidate order on ties; `none` means wait. The tick tie-break guarantees that following it reaches `done` even where waiting loops (he reaches forever). A state the search didn't expand — or, after an incomplete search, one with no known way to the goal — gets a fresh `solve` from it with the same options plus `stopAtGoal`. (8) **Errors.** A `CascadeOverflowError` from `step` ends the search with `stop: 'error'` and `error: { kind: 'cascadeOverflow', message, path }` (lint rule 12); any other error propagates. (9) **Clock.** The core may not read the wall clock (boundary test), so `timeMs` and `ms` need an injected `clock` (the hint worker passes `() => performance.now()`); without one, `ms` is null and `timeMs` is ignored. The clock is read every 256 expansions. (10) **Memory.** `Map<string, number>` from key to vertex, everything else in growable `Int32Array`s (moves, parent, parent input, edge ranges, edge targets and input ids into a small table of distinct inputs) plus a `Uint8Array` of flags; states are kept only while queued. **Why.** These are the cheapest readings that keep the answers exact: dropping only fields with no effect keeps the graph a true quotient; offering every player gesture (taps on tabs too) makes "from every reachable state" honest; the lexicographic (moves, ticks) cost is what makes `nextAction` safe to follow blindly, which hints need. Throughput is ~7,000 states/s (≈ 10 `step` calls per state), dominated by `step` itself.
**Consequences.** #87's `pages.test.ts` asserts `solvable && deadEndCount === 0 && !trivial && mischiefReachable && tudigongReachable` per page and prints `deadEnds[0].path` via `toSolutionSteps` on failure. #88's CLI reports par, `statesExplored`, `ms` (pass a clock), dead ends and the reachability flags. #100's worker calls `solve(def, current, { cap: 200_000, timeMs: 300, clock, stopAtGoal: true })` and uses `nextAction(current)`; on `stop: 'time'` without a solution it falls back as GDD § 2.7 says.    - **Parts.** Part tweens use an ease-out-back tuned so the peak is exactly 1.06 (c ≈ 1.29); the "2 px settle" is that curve's return. The 60 ms 0.97 squash follows, about the part's centre. Transforms only.
   - **Rides.** An attached node is drawn at its part's tween; an actor stepping onto it lerps to that moving point.
   - **Reduced motion:** instant swaps.
5. **Animated-node budget.** It is counted strictly: every element inside a `data-anim` group, not just the groups.
   - To fit, `depth()` puts a single-element outline's copy attributes on the element itself, and stand-ins draw one path per colour.
   - The worst frame of 1-2 is ≤ 120 nodes in 8–9 moving groups. The render test holds every page to ≤ 150.
6. **Session.**
   - **Pause** is by named reason. `elapsed()` stands still while paused, so the view freezes mid-step.
   - **Resume** runs only after a 150 ms grace with no reason left; a new pause cancels the grace.
   - **Fast-forward:** while Baokaka is `moving` with more than 4 edges left on his BFS path and nothing is queued, a tick is a quarter long. A queued input ends it at once.
   - **Done:** at `done` the clock stops scheduling ticks but keeps time, so the last frame plays out.
7. **Registry.** Unknown part keys fall back to the preset's kit art, unknown backdrop keys to `placeholderBackdrop`, unknown creature keys to `origamiBird`. Each missing key is `console.info`-logged once per kind in dev builds.
8. **Dev parameters** on `#/popup?page=<id>`: `tick=N`, `paused=1`, `speed=` and `demo=1` (three idle ticks, then the authored solution through the session). Production will open the save's page (#93).

**Why.** Reading the scenery off the walk graph means every page is legible the moment its data exists, and the drawing can never disagree with where he can go. Splitting parts by z is the only order in which a train can slide behind a platform. Counting the budget strictly leaves headroom for the real bitmaps, which replace whole subtrees with one `<image>`.

**Consequences.**

### D-51 · 2026-10-19 · Gestures: the readings #90 left open, and R6 on short phones
**Decision.** (#90.) `src/popup/view/gestures.ts` (pure) and `useGestures.ts` (Pointer Events plumbing in `PagePlayer`):
1. **R6 on short phones (the #89 note).** At 375 × 667 the book is 0.66 scale and 1-2's train tab hit (56 units) is ≈ 37 px. Every touchable's hit shape is **grown about its centre to 48 × 48 screen px after the book scale** (`minHit`), on top of the 32 px magnet. The page data keeps its page-unit hits (lint still asks ≥ 48 units); the growth is a view rule, so it holds at any viewport. A test checks every touchable on every page, every frame of its solution, at 390 × 844 and 375 × 667. #97's compact HUD may still let the book grow on short screens; it is no longer needed for R6.
2. **Hit-test order.** The topmost target whose own shape contains the point; else the grown shapes that contain it (nearest own shape; ties to the higher layer); else the magnet: the nearest grown shape within 32 screen px (ties to the higher layer).
3. **Targets.** Player-driven parts (input ≠ `none`) by their `hit`; perch paw prints (r 24, while Mocha is on the page, not his own perch); Mocha (56 × 70 above his feet) and Baokaka (96 × 66) on the actors' layers; the 土地公 (r 22) while `visibleWhen` holds. The 土地公 is drawn far back but sits **above the parts** for touch, because he is a small find hidden behind parts (1-2's board).
4. **Taps.** Under 8 px of travel. A tap-only target (prop, perch, Mocha, Baokaka) also takes a wobbly tap if the finger lifts within the magnet of it. Tab → `tap` (advance), wheel → `tap` (clockwise), flap/prop/light → `tap`, perch → `mocha`, Mocha → `pet`. **Baokaka and the 土地公 are game events, not sim inputs**: `wordRing` (the stub dims the page and circles him, `pause('ring')`; any touch closes it; #99) and `tudigong` (#101 awards the sticker). The 250 ms debounce is per target.
5. **Drags.** The magnet also picks the part a drag starts on. Linear drags are relative to where the finger landed, projected on the track, scaled to stops; over-drag eases toward 0.35 stop past the ends; release snaps to the nearest stop and queues `set`. Rotary drags accumulate angle (clockwise on screen = the listed state order) and queue `set` to the wrapped stop (the core walks to it stop by stop). A flap drag lifts by distance / reach and toggles (queues `tap`) past half. Riders (the train's nodes, Baokaka on it) follow the preview.
6. **Commit and settle.** The input applies at the next tick boundary like any other. Until that tick the part is held at its snapped stop, then glides (the part tween's curve) to where the state really landed, so a jam at `half` reads as a glide back, never a jump.
7. **Locked parts** answer in the view at once (the world is paused under the finger): `locked` flag with a wobble, Mocha's glare, a `nope` event; nothing is queued, so the core's `locked` event is not needed for this path.
8. **Touch pauses the world.** The whole screen takes pointers; any finger down pauses (`pause('finger')`), the last up resumes after the session's 150 ms grace. Only the first finger drives a gesture; more than 3 points at once cancels it. Baokaka tilts 3° toward the finger (`data-watch`).
9. **Hold-to-reveal.** 600 ms still (under 8 px) on empty space: every touchable's own hit shape gets a 2 px dashed chalk outline until release.
10. **State flags** for #140: `data-dragging`, `data-locked`, `data-glare`, `data-watch`, `data-reveal`; the page SVG also carries `data-parts` and `data-done` for tools.

**Why.** Growing hits after the scale answers R6 everywhere with no layout dependency and no data churn, and the hit layer draws the same grown shapes the hit test uses. Answering locks in the view keeps feedback immediate while the world is paused, and keeps the one-input-per-tick queue for real moves.

### D-52 · 2026-10-19 · PageLab: the readings #91 left open
**Decision.** (#91, GDD § 3.10.)
1. **Its own stepper, not `PageSession`.** The lab must load any state (a dead end, a solve's start), step exactly one tick, and keep a log, so `dev/labModel.ts` steps `core/step` directly (`labLoad`, `labStep`) and keeps the last 20 ticks' inputs and events. A step that throws (a reaction loop) leaves the state and shows the error. Ticks animate like the game's (`PageView` given the time since the tick). The palette is `candidateInputs` without the wait: exactly what the solver tries, so "every legal input" means the same thing in both.
2. **The solver worker is PageLab's own** (`dev/solver.worker.ts` over `dev/solverJob.ts`), not #100's hint worker, which doesn't exist yet; PageLab may switch later. On load it posts the report of every lab page (`pages/report.ts`'s `reportPage`, the same judge as `pages.test.ts` and `npm run solve`) for the badges; a solve posts `SolveResult` without `nextAction` (structured clone). An edited page is sent as data; a newer request supersedes an older one's answer.
3. **Badges:** the worst problem wins — `over cap`, then `dead-end`, `lint`, any other failure (`fail`), else `ok`; `pending` until the report arrives. The badge judges the registered page; the lint panel judges the edited copy, live.
4. **Lab fixtures.** The page list ends with pages broken on purpose (`dev/labPages.ts`: `c1p2-broken`, 1-2 whose train locks for good if Mocha scares the pigeon while it is still `out`), so the issue's manual test — load a dead end from a broken 1-2 — runs on real data. They never enter the registry or the game.
5. **Overlays.** Six checkboxes: `nodes`, `edges` (Baokaka's kinds), `perches` (with the cat edges `jump`/`squeeze`, and a dotted line to each `reachNode`), `attractors` (rank in § 2.2.2 order and priority; faded when he can't want it now), `hits` (the data shape and the grown 48 px shape, D-51) and `target` (line, path preview to the nearest reachable node, Mocha's trip). Edit mode always shows the nodes. Diagram colours are a dev-only `PAGELAB` group in `palette.ts` (the "no raw hex in src/popup" rule holds).
6. **Edit mode** moves positions only (GDD: structural edits stay in code): a free node's `x`/`y`, a riding node's `attach.pos` for its part's **current** state, and either end of a part's `track`; values round to whole units. 「Copy TS」 writes the `nodes` block and one `track:` line per part (under a `// part '…'` comment) in the page files' style; the page files' inline comments aren't kept.
7. **URL.** `page`, `overlay` (absent = `nodes,edges,target`; empty = none), `tick` (0–500 idle ticks, like `#/popup`), `speed`, `edit=1`, `view=grey,squint`, `section=kit`. Changes rewrite the hash with `replaceState` (no history entry per checkbox); a new `page` or `tick` reloads the page and drops edits.
8. **Readability (bible § 4.2).** Greyscale is a CSS filter on the page only; squint is a second `PageView` at 0.25 scale; the count is touchable parts plus perches in the current state (`targetsOf`), flagged over 3 on pages 1–3 of a chapter. The art-key report calls a key "baked" when the registry has its own art for it (`isPlaceholder`, `hasBackdropArt`, `hasCreatureArt`), else "placeholder"; everything is a placeholder in M0. #140's affordance looks will show up in these views as they land.

**Why.** Designers live in this tool (GDD § 6 risk 2): one link reproduces a view, the solver never freezes the page, and the dead-end tools can be exercised before a real page has a dead end.
